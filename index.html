Working Papers

  • Fairness and Cooperation: Draft Dodging by the Rich and War Morale in World War II
    Revise & Resubmit, Journal of Political Economy
    [Abstract]
    This paper demonstrates that draft avoidance by the rich undermined war morale in the US during WWII. Using newly digitized individual-level draft records, I show that men whose richer neighbors received more draft exemptions volunteered less, filed more draft appeals, and deserted more. To identify the causal effect, I use an instrument based on randomly assigned draft lottery numbers. By randomly setting the sequence of case reviews, the lottery gave some men more time to prepare their case for an exemption. Heterogeneity analyses and evidence from local newspapers are consistent with a mechanism where individuals perceived the unequal, class-based sharing of the wartime burden as unfair.
